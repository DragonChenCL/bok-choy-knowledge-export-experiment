# Research Log — 2026-09-07

## Objective
Validate whether **Bok Choy pest diagnosis / rescue knowledge** deserves a paid-knowledge-product experiment for overseas home gardeners.

## Logging format
Each step records: action → evidence → observation → inference → next action.

---

## Step 1 — Confirm that the problem exists in current user discussions

### Action
Search current public web results for Reddit discussions around bok choy being eaten, leaf holes, and pest confusion.

### Evidence
Observed current/recent Reddit discussions surfaced through search, including examples where users ask what is eating bok choy, cannot find the pest, or are unsure how to respond after trying home remedies.

### Observation
The recurring user language is symptom-first, not taxonomy-first. Users tend to ask questions like:
- what is eating my bok choy?
- why are there holes in the leaves?
- I cannot see the pest — what should I look for?
- is the plant still worth saving?

### Inference
A guide organized around **visible damage → likely cause → how to confirm → what to do next** is more aligned with user behavior than a conventional pest encyclopedia organized alphabetically by pest name.

### Next action
Cross-check the likely pest set against authoritative agricultural sources.

---

## Step 2 — Cross-check common bok choy / brassica pest categories

### Action
Search university Extension and agricultural sources for bok choy / pak choi / brassica pest guidance.

### Evidence
Current university / Extension material consistently identifies common brassica and Asian-green problems that include aphids, flea beetles, caterpillars/cabbage worms, slugs/snails, and related chewing or sap-feeding pests.

### Observation
These categories map well to symptom-led diagnostic patterns such as:
- many tiny shot holes
- larger ragged holes
- leaf-edge or skeletonized chewing
- curled/sticky growth with clustered insects
- seedlings damaged near ground level or overnight

### Inference
The product can be useful without pretending to provide definitive image-based diagnosis. It can instead teach the reader to inspect the plant and narrow likely causes using visible patterns and confirmation checks.

### Next action
Validate whether consumers already pay for this kind of decision aid in printable/PDF form.

---

## Step 3 — Check paid-product evidence

### Action
Search Etsy and other digital-product marketplaces for garden pest identification, diagnostic flowcharts, printable pest guides, and similar products.

### Evidence
Current Etsy listings include paid digital garden-pest identification and diagnostic printables, including products positioned as quick-reference decision aids rather than long textbooks.

### Observation
The commercial format exists: users are offered printable charts, flowcharts, identification guides, and pest-control references as digital downloads.

### Inference
This supports **format-level payment evidence** (people are offered and review/buy this kind of digital guide), but it does **not yet prove** that a Bok Choy-only paid guide has sufficient demand.

### Next action
Do not create the paid PDF yet. First build a keyword / pain-point map specific to Bok Choy and identify the strongest problem cluster.

---

## Step 4 — Product-scope decision

### Candidate scopes considered
1. General garden pest guide
2. Leafy greens pest guide
3. Asian greens pest guide
4. Bok Choy pest rescue guide

### Data-supported reasoning
- General garden pest content has abundant existing supply and established competitors.
- Asian greens provides more specificity but still spans several crops.
- Bok Choy has repeated symptom-led community questions and offers a narrow enough scope to test cheaply.
- Paid printable pest guides prove the format, while Bok Choy specificity gives us a potential information-organization advantage.

### Decision
Proceed with **EXP-001: Bok Choy Pest Rescue** as the first validation unit.

### Confidence
Medium. Problem evidence is good; Bok Choy-specific willingness-to-pay is not yet proven.

### Required next evidence
- search-intent / keyword map
- SERP composition for symptom-led queries
- 30–50 real user questions grouped by cluster
- competing paid product structure and pricing
- evidence that the final guide can add decision value beyond free Extension articles

---

## Current hypothesis
A beginner home gardener may pay for a compact visual guide if it reduces the time and uncertainty involved in answering:

> “What is damaging my bok choy, how can I confirm it, and what should I do next?”

This remains a hypothesis, not a conclusion.
