# EXP-003 — Solution Evidence Matrix v0

Date: 2026-09-07
Purpose: separate user-pain evidence from solution authority before writing the paid product.

## Rule
Community posts and comments are excellent for discovering **what users experience**. They are not automatically reliable sources for **why it happens** or **how to fix it**.

Every technical statement in Bao Rescue must have a confidence label.

---

## Claim family A — Condensation can damage steamed-bun surface / make buns wet

### Evidence
**King Arthur Baking — `Everything you need to know to make steamed buns`**
https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2022/10/17/everything-you-need-to-know-to-make-steamed-buns

Observed guidance:
- bamboo steamers absorb condensation;
- this helps prevent dripping water from making buns unpleasantly wet.

### Status
**Supported — high confidence for moisture / surface-wetness risk.**

### Product wording allowed
`Condensation is worth checking when the bun surface is wet, soggy, pitted or visibly water-marked.`

### Product wording NOT yet allowed
`Condensation is the main cause of every wrinkled bun.`

Reason: wrinkling has multiple plausible cause families in the literature / expert guides.

## Claim family B — Rapid lid opening / cooling can contribute to collapse

### Evidence 1
**King Arthur Baking**
- advises resisting the urge to open the steamer during cooking;
- says a rush of cool air could potentially make yeasted buns collapse;
- recommends leaving yeasted buns covered for about 5 minutes after heat is turned off.

### Evidence 2
**Omnivore's Cookbook — `How to Make Steamed Bao Buns`**
https://omnivorescookbook.com/steamed-bao-buns/

Observed guidance:
- discusses sudden pressure changes during steaming;
- recommends gradual temperature / pressure transition and resting before opening.

### Status
**Supported — medium/high confidence as one collapse pathway.**

### Product wording allowed
`If the buns looked inflated until the lid was opened, a sudden temperature / steam transition is one important path to check.`

### Product wording NOT allowed
`Opening the lid too fast is always the reason Bao collapses.`

Reason: over-proofing and structural factors are also repeatedly identified.

## Claim family C — Over-proofing can cause deflation / collapse

### Evidence
**Omnivore's Cookbook**
- explicitly states buns deflating after steaming is usually caused by over-proofing in its method;
- notes proofing time varies with environment and ingredient conditions.

### Status
**Supported — high confidence within comparable yeasted Bao methods.**

### Product wording allowed
`Over-proofing is a major cause family to check when buns expand strongly and then lose structure.`

### Important limitation
Do not convert this into a universal minute-based proofing rule.

---

## Claim family D — Proofing time is environment-dependent

### Evidence 1
**Omnivore's Cookbook**
- explicitly notes proof time can vary substantially depending on environment and ingredient conditions such as yeast freshness.

### Evidence 2
**Red House Spice — complete Bao guide**
https://redhousespice.com/steamed-bao-buns/

Observed context:
- properly risen, smooth, fluffy Bao wrappers are described as one of the tricky parts of the process;
- large reader-comment history contains repeated proofing and outcome questions.

### Status
**Supported — high confidence.**

### Product implication
The guide should teach **observable proof state**, not promise `proof exactly 30 minutes`.

## Claim family E — Dense / not-fluffy results have multiple root causes

### Evidence
Current English troubleshooting pages repeatedly associate dense / heavy results with combinations of:
- insufficient gas generation / inactive yeast;
- under-proofing;
- dough structure / kneading problems;
- flour differences;
- collapse after an initially successful rise.

Sources observed include:
- Dished by Kate — https://dishedbykate.com/how-to-make-bao-buns/
- Omnivore's Cookbook — https://omnivorescookbook.com/steamed-bao-buns/
- CharWok — https://charwok.com/steamed-bao-buns/

### Status
**Supported as a multi-cause diagnostic cluster.**

### Product implication
Never write `dense buns = dead yeast` as a one-step diagnosis.
The user must move through a decision tree.

## Claim family F — No-yeast steamed-bun advice can conflict with yeasted Bao advice

### Evidence
**What To Cook Today — no-yeast steamed buns**
https://whattocooktoday.com/no-yeast-chinese-steamed-buns.html

Its troubleshooting specifically describes high heat as beneficial for that **no-yeast, chemical-leavening** method while noting no-yeast buns behave differently from regular yeasted buns.

### Status
**Critical scope distinction — high confidence.**

### Product implication
Do NOT merge advice across:
- yeasted Bao / Baozi;
- no-yeast / baking-powder-only buns;
- every regional Mantou formula.

MVP scope must remain explicit.

## Claim family G — `thermal shock` is commonly used as an explanation, but wording needs discipline

### Evidence
Multiple current recipe / troubleshooting pages attribute collapse or wrinkling to sudden temperature / pressure changes.

### Status
**Useful explanatory shorthand, but mechanistic precision is not yet sufficient for overconfident scientific claims.**

### Product wording allowed
`A sudden transition in the steamer environment is one common failure path described by established Bao guides.`

### Avoid
`The gas molecules contract by X% and therefore the bun collapses.`

unless a food-science source directly supports the specific mechanism and context.

---

## Community-only observations — useful for discovery, not solution proof

Examples from Reddit / comments:
- one bun wrinkles while others in the same batch remain fine;
- users suspect condensation;
- users open the lid and observe collapse;
- some users repeatedly change flour / proof time / steamer and still fail;
- users describe pasta-like, lumpy, dense and wet textures.

Use these to shape symptom language.
Do not cite community vote counts as scientific evidence.

---

## Evidence gaps before final paid copy

1. Better food-science support for how proofing state affects steamed-bread structure.
2. Better controlled evidence separating steam intensity from proofing as causes of wrinkling.
3. Clearer evidence on flour protein range and Bao texture across US / UK flour categories.
4. Safe, limited localization notes for active dry vs instant yeast.
5. Decide whether filled Baozi and folded Gua Bao can share the same troubleshooting branches or need scope separation.

## Current publication rule
Until gaps are resolved:
- strong evidence → state directly;
- multiple credible cause families → use decision-tree language;
- community-only theory → label as possibility or exclude;
- conflicting method families → separate explicitly.
