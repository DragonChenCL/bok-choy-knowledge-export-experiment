# EXP-003 — Diagnostic Evidence Matrix v4

Date: 2026-09-08
Status: first-pass evidence mapping for product logic

## Purpose
Map each commercial diagnostic claim to evidence strength before it is allowed into v4 copy.

Evidence grades:
- **A** = multiple independent strong sources and/or experimental/mechanistic support
- **B** = one strong specialist source + compatible general mechanism
- **C** = anecdotal, recipe-specific, conflicting, or transfer risk
- **UNKNOWN** = insufficient evidence

---

## E01 — Proofing should be judged by dough state, not clock alone

**Claim**
Room/dough temperature changes proofing speed, so fixed minutes are only a guide. Useful observable cues include increased volume/plumpness, lighter feel, and finger-press recovery.

**Grade: A**

**Evidence**
1. What To Cook Today: shaped steamed buns are described as roughly 50% larger, lighter, and slowly springing back when correctly proofed; immediate rebound indicates more proofing needed; no rebound indicates over-proofing.
   - https://whattocooktoday.com/soft-fluffy-asian-steamed-buns-every-time.html
2. Red House Spice: bao are ready when visibly plumper/bigger (not doubled) and lighter; proof time changes with room temperature.
   - https://redhousespice.com/char-siu-bao/
   - https://redhousespice.com/bao/
3. King Arthur general yeast guidance: rise should be judged by progress, not fixed time; temperature materially affects fermentation rate.
   - https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2023/05/10/guide-to-baking-with-yeast
   - https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2023/03/23/ask-the-bread-coach-my-dough-isnt-rising-what-now

**Commercial wording allowed**
Strong.

## E02 — Under-proofing can produce dense/heavy steamed buns

**Grade: A/B**

**Evidence**
1. What To Cook Today explicitly associates under-proofed buns with dense/bumpy results.
   - https://whattocooktoday.com/soft-fluffy-asian-steamed-buns-every-time.html
2. King Arthur general yeast-bread guidance says stiff/dry dough and inadequate rise produce denser results.
   - https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/learn/guides/yeast-baking
3. Chinese steamed bread research links fermentation/gas production and gluten structure to specific volume and pore structure.
   - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6712117/

**Commercial wording allowed**
Yes, but not as the only possible cause of dense texture.

---

## E03 — Over-proofing can weaken structure and lead to spreading/collapse/wrinkling

**Grade: A**

**Evidence**
1. Red House Spice controlled recipe comparison: 30-min rested mantou remained smooth; 45-min over-rested example formed a large internal air pocket and collapsed after steaming.
   - https://redhousespice.com/mantou-steamed-buns/
2. What To Cook Today repeatedly identifies over-proofing with collapse/wrinkle and sideways spread.
   - https://whattocooktoday.com/soft-fluffy-asian-steamed-buns-every-time.html
3. King Arthur yeast guide explains that prolonged fermentation can weaken gluten/gas retention.
   - https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2023/05/10/guide-to-baking-with-yeast

**Commercial wording allowed**
Strong when user observations also support over-proofing.

---

## E04 — A short post-steam covered rest can reduce sudden-temperature shrinkage

**Grade: C / CONFLICTED**

**Supporting evidence**
1. 2026 steamed stuffed bun experimental process used a 3-minute standing period before removal specifically to prevent surface shrinkage caused by sudden temperature changes.
   - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12907837/
2. What To Cook Today recommends ~3 minutes before opening and treats sudden temperature change as a possible contributor.
   - https://whattocooktoday.com/soft-fluffy-asian-steamed-buns-every-time.html

**Conflicting evidence**
Red House Spice states that after numerous tests, waiting before uncovering had no value when their mantou dough was properly prepared/proofed; immediate lid removal worked.
- https://redhousespice.com/mantou-steamed-buns/

**Commercial wording allowed**
Only as a secondary hypothesis / controlled A-B test after proofing is controlled.

**Forbidden wording**
- “Opening the lid too soon is the cause.”
- “Always wait X minutes or your bao will collapse.”

## E05 — Condensation dripping from the lid can create wet/soggy/appearance damage

**Grade: A**

**Evidence**
1. King Arthur: bamboo absorbs condensation; metal steamer lid should be wrapped to prevent droplets falling onto buns.
   - https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2022/10/17/everything-you-need-to-know-to-make-steamed-buns
2. Red House Spice recommends cloth over metal/plastic steamer lid to absorb condensation because dripping water can affect bao appearance.
   - https://redhousespice.com/char-siu-bao/
3. What To Cook Today gives the same practical prevention.
   - https://whattocooktoday.com/soft-fluffy-asian-steamed-buns-every-time.html

**Commercial wording allowed**
Strong when visible moisture/drip evidence is present.

---

## E06 — Flour/hydration differences can materially affect steamed-bun texture and volume

**Grade: A/B**

**Evidence**
1. King Arthur hydration guide: different flours absorb water differently; drier/stiffer dough tends toward denser/drier texture and slower rise.
   - https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2023/01/11/bread-hydration
2. King Arthur yeast-baking guide: too much flour makes dough/bread dry and dense; stiffer dough tends toward denser texture.
   - https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/learn/guides/yeast-baking
3. Chinese steamed bread literature consistently links gluten/protein network, flour composition and water relations to specific volume/texture.
   - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7037005/
   - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9351791/
   - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12621561/

**Commercial wording allowed**
Yes, especially when user changed flour or reports unusually stiff/dry dough.

**Caution**
Do not translate regional flour labels as exact equivalents solely from name (US AP, UK plain, Chinese bao flour, Italian 00, etc.).

---

## E07 — Yeast type, temperature and activity affect rise rate

**Grade: A**

**Evidence**
1. King Arthur: yeast activity depends on warmth; active dry can start more slowly than instant; cold dough requires more time.
   - https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2023/05/10/guide-to-baking-with-yeast
2. King Arthur desired dough temperature article discusses instant vs active-dry handling differences under cool-water conditions.
   - https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2018/05/29/desired-dough-temperature
3. Red House Spice/What To Cook Today both tell users to adjust proof time to actual temperature and dough condition.

**Commercial wording allowed**
Yes.

**Caution**
Do not prescribe one universal water temperature across every yeast manufacturer/formula.

---

## E08 — Insufficient dough/gluten development can contribute to crumbly/cakey/weak structure

**Grade: B**

**Evidence**
1. What To Cook Today identifies insufficient gluten development and overly dry dough as causes of crumbly/cakey buns.
   - https://whattocooktoday.com/soft-fluffy-asian-steamed-buns-every-time.html
2. Chinese steamed bread research links continuous/extensible protein networks and gluten structure to final texture/quality.
   - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7037005/

**Commercial wording allowed**
Moderate, when dough-state evidence supports it.

---

## E09 — Wet filling / thin wrapper can contribute to local wrinkling or wrapper problems

**Grade: C**

**Evidence**
What To Cook Today reports wet filling as a wrinkle/collapse contributor and separately warns that wrappers rolled too thin can wrinkle around filling.
- https://whattocooktoday.com/soft-fluffy-asian-steamed-buns-every-time.html

**Commercial wording allowed**
Secondary only; needs more independent source support.

---

## E10 — “Cracked top” is not universally a defect

**Grade: A for scope caution**

**Evidence**
Some Cantonese char siu bao intentionally use formulation/shaping that encourages characteristic cracking during steaming.
- https://whattocooktoday.com/barbecued-pork-steamed-bun-char-siu-bao.html

**Commercial implication**
Ask intended bao style before diagnosing cracks.

---

## E11 — Surface drying / aggressive steam / shaping bubbles can affect smoothness

**Grade: B/C depending subclaim**

**Evidence**
1. What To Cook Today advises cold/room-temperature liquid in its method to prevent premature yeast activity and shaping bubbles, medium heat for smoother buns, and covering/handling to prevent rough surface.
   - https://whattocooktoday.com/soft-fluffy-asian-steamed-buns-every-time.html
2. Red House Spice emphasizes covering unused dough to prevent drying.
   - https://redhousespice.com/red-bean-buns/

**Commercial wording allowed**
Secondary, recipe-specific. Do not outrank clearer proofing/condensation evidence.

# Contradiction register

## C-001 — Lid rest after steaming
Status: OPEN / CONFLICTED.

Do not resolve by averaging sources. Turn the conflict into a user-level controlled experiment after proofing state is stabilized.

## C-002 — Exact ideal proof percentage
Several specialist sources use ~50% increase as a practical cue for their formulas, but this should be presented as a practical reference, not a universal laboratory threshold for all bao recipes.

## C-003 — Flour labels across countries
Do not create direct equivalence tables without protein/specification data.
