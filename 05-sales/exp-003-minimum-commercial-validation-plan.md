# EXP-003 — Minimum Commercial Validation Plan

Date: 2026-09-07
Status: approved to prepare, not yet live

## Objective
Validate whether a stranger with a real steamed-bun failure problem will show commercial intent toward a focused troubleshooting product.

This stage does **not** attempt to prove a large business. It attempts to answer one question:

> Will qualified users click toward checkout, join an early-access list, or pay for a compact Bao/steamed-bun troubleshooting system?

## Evidence that justifies moving beyond desk research

### 1. Large enough parent market
- Public Semrush page observed `bao buns` at about 60,500 US monthly searches (June 2026). This is parent-market evidence only, not failure-query volume.
- Multiple English Bao tutorials have seven-figure YouTube views.

### 2. Repeated failure pain is real and current
Across Reddit, recipe comments, and cooking communities, repeated failure clusters include:
- wrinkling / collapse after steaming;
- dense / doughy / not fluffy texture;
- uncertainty about under-proofing vs correct proof vs over-proofing;
- condensation / steamer / lid-opening problems;
- flour, yeast, hydration and local-kitchen differences.

Recent examples include 2026 Reddit posts with substantial engagement and current recipe pages with troubleshooting sections and large comment histories.

### 3. Existing free content is fragmented
Current SERPs often place troubleshooting inside:
- recipe FAQs;
- blog sections;
- forum / Reddit threads;
- individual videos.

There is no dominant dedicated Bao failure-diagnosis brand observed in the current manual review.

### 4. Adjacent payment evidence exists
Observed current digital-product examples:
- generic Bao recipe PDF: about $4.53 on Etsy;
- sourdough troubleshooting PDFs commonly around $4.99–$8.99;
- `Revive Sourdough Starter Guide / 3-Day Reset Plan`: about $6.95;
- `Zefir Troubleshooting Guide`: around $10+;
- interactive sourdough troubleshooting systems can sell at materially higher prices.

This supports testing a troubleshooting-specific digital product above a bare recipe-card price, but does **not** prove Bao troubleshooting willingness-to-pay.

## Offer hypothesis

### Working name
**Bao Rescue — Why Did My Steamed Buns Fail?**

### Promise
A visual troubleshooting guide that helps a beginner move from:

`what I see` → `what to check` → `most likely cause` → `what to change next batch`

without reading ten recipe blogs or guessing from conflicting comments.

### Initial scope
Only the three strongest recurring failure families:
1. **Wrinkled / collapsed buns**
2. **Dense / tough / not fluffy buns**
3. **Proofing uncertainty**

Secondary quick-reference coverage:
- condensation / steamer behavior;
- flour / yeast / hydration checks;
- common surface / color symptoms.

## Product format hypothesis

**8–12 page visual PDF + 1-page printable quick diagnostic.**

Why this format:
- existing paid adjacent products validate small downloadable PDFs;
- user pain is diagnostic rather than encyclopedic;
- current SERP answers are fragmented, so the value proposition is compression and decision flow;
- keeping version 1 small minimizes sunk cost before willingness-to-pay is demonstrated.

## Price hypothesis

**Launch test price: USD $6.90**

Evidence basis:
- generic Bao PDF observed at ~$4.53;
- adjacent troubleshooting PDFs often appear around ~$5–$9;
- stronger diagnostic systems can price materially higher.

Classification: **HYPOTHESIS**, not market fact.

## Commercial-test sequence

### Stage A — Build the real minimum product
Create the evidence-backed 8–12 page guide and 1-page printable.
Do not create a 50–100 page ebook.

### Stage B — Create one focused sales page
The sales page must sell the outcome, not page count:
- identify the failure;
- separate likely causes;
- know what to change next batch;
- avoid repeating the same failed bake.

### Stage C — Put a real checkout behind the CTA
No fake checkout button.
If payment is collected, the product must be deliverable immediately.

### Stage D — Drive only qualified traffic
Initial distribution should target problem-aware users through:
- SEO pages for exact failure intents;
- Pinterest diagnostic graphics;
- helpful Reddit / recipe-community participation where rules allow;
- short-form symptom-first content if useful.

### Stage E — Observe, do not rationalize
Track at minimum:
- qualified visits;
- CTA clicks;
- checkout starts (if platform exposes them);
- purchases;
- refund / complaint signals;
- which failure-intent page produced the visit.

## Internal test thresholds
These are experiment operating rules, **not industry facts**.

- **First meaningful milestone:** 1 purchase from a stranger.
- If 100+ qualified visitors produce zero checkout intent, review positioning / product before adding more content.
- If users click but do not buy, investigate price, trust, preview quality and product promise.
- If SEO / social cannot produce qualified traffic, do not blame the product before separating distribution failure from offer failure.

## Stop conditions
Stop or pivot if one of the following becomes clear:
- users want recipes, not troubleshooting;
- the failure cluster is too small to acquire traffic economically;
- existing free pages satisfy the problem so well that the compact product adds little decision value;
- product credibility cannot be established without pretending to have experience we do not have;
- paid intent remains absent after a reasonable qualified-traffic test.

## Evidence status
- Parent market size: **FACT / third-party estimate**
- Real failure pain: **OBSERVATION, high confidence**
- Dedicated competition appears weak: **OBSERVATION, medium confidence**
- Users will pay $6.90 for this exact Bao product: **HYPOTHESIS**
- Exact failure-query search volume: **UNKNOWN**
