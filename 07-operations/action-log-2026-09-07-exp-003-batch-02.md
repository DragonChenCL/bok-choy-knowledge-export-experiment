Are bao / steamed-bun failures repeated across several failure types, or did we overreact to one viral wrinkling post?

- wrinkled buns
- collapse
- dense / not fluffy
- proofing

The problem is broader than “why did one bun wrinkle?” and resembles a multi-variable home-process diagnosis problem.

### Confidence
Medium.

### Decision
Continue validation. Do not build yet.

## Action 4 — Validate cross-market source advantage

### Hypothesis / question
Does Chinese steamed-bread research materially deepen our possible solution quality?

### Action
Searched Chinese steamed bread / mantou food-science research around proofing, structure, temperature, humidity, fermentation, cooling and texture.

### Raw evidence
Found academic evidence that:
- proofing time and temperature materially affect dough and final texture;
- insufficient and excessive fermentation produce different quality failures;
- flour composition, leavening, fermentation conditions and steaming protocols contribute to regional texture differences;
- fermentation tolerance and pore stability are active research topics;
- cooling and water migration after steaming affect texture;
- pH / acidification affects gluten structure and final texture.

### Observation
The Chinese knowledge base is deeper and more process-oriented than typical English recipe FAQs.

### Interpretation
Cross-market knowledge transfer is a plausible differentiation source, provided claims are translated and localized rather than copied.

### Confidence
High for source-depth advantage; low/medium for commercial advantage.

### Decision
Continue EXP-003 and maintain localization as a mandatory research layer.
