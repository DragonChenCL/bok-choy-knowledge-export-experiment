How to narrow it

Use the timing and the strongest visible clue.

1. Spreading during final proof?

Very soft dough or an advanced proof state can reduce shape retention. Do not add flour automatically; first compare dough feel and proof endpoint.

2. Tall before steaming, flat afterward?

That overlaps the collapse branch. Strong over-proof cues or post-steam structure loss are more relevant than shaping alone.

3. Dough much softer than usual?

Hydration changes gluten-network structure and steamed-bread quality. A measured formula difference is stronger evidence than the final flat shape by itself.

Next-batch rule

Photograph the buns immediately after shaping and again just before steaming. If they spread during proof, keep the formula fixed and test an earlier proof endpoint. If they stay tall until steaming, use the collapse diagnosis instead.

FAQ

Quick answers

Why do my bao spread during proofing?

A very soft dough or a late proof state can contribute, but the timing of the spread is more useful than the final shape alone.

Is sideways spread the same as collapse?

Not always. If the bun spreads before steaming, diagnose proofing and dough feel first. If it loses volume after steaming, use the collapse branch.

Should I add more flour?

Only if you have a direct softness or measurement clue. Random flour changes can create a new problem.
