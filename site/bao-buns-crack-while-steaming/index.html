Bao troubleshooting

Why do my bao buns crack while steaming?

First decide whether the opening is a style feature or an unwanted failure. A naturally split char siu-style top is not the same problem as a plain smooth bun tearing during proof or steaming.

How to narrow it

Use the timing and the strongest visible clue.

1. Cracked before steaming?

That points toward shaping, surface drying, or dough stiffness rather than steam pressure alone. Check whether the dough could form a smooth skin without tearing.

2. Smooth before steaming, split during steaming?

Record proof state and dough feel. A crack by itself is not enough evidence to name one cause, so keep this branch conservative.

3. Was the dough unusually firm?

Flour absorbs water differently by brand. A medium-firm, smooth dough is a common target; a clearly over-firm dough is a stronger clue than the crack alone.

Next-batch rule

For a plain smooth-bun style, keep proofing and steam time fixed and correct only a clearly dry/stiff dough in a measured amount. If the dough felt normal, collect one more batch of evidence before changing the formula.

FAQ

Quick answers

Are cracks always a failure?

No. Some bao styles are intentionally open or split. Diagnose cracking only when the intended style should be smooth.

Does cracking mean the dough is too dry?

Dry or stiff dough is one clue, not a universal diagnosis. The timing of the crack and dough feel matter.

What should I change first?

Only correct hydration when the dough was clearly too firm. Otherwise preserve the formula and collect more evidence from proof state and crack timing.
