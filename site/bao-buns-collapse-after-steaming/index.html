Failure timing matters

Why do bao buns collapse after steaming?

If your buns looked inflated and then deflated, do not jump straight to one explanation. The two strongest branches to check are proofing state and the transition out of the steamer.

First question: when did they collapse?

They were fragile before steaming

Over-proofing moves higher on the list, especially if the buns had spread wider, felt extremely airy, or a fingertip indentation did not recover.

They looked good until the lid opened

Check the post-steam transition as a separate variable. A rapid move from hot steam to cool room air is a documented shrinkage / collapse pathway in established guidance and experimental process design.

They are also wet or pitted

Condensation may be contributing to surface damage. Look for direct water droplets from the lid.

They barely puffed at all

This is less like a classic post-steam collapse problem and more like yeast activity, under-proofing, temperature, or dough-structure troubleshooting.

A simple decision sequence

Judge the final proof state

Do not rely on minutes alone. Room temperature, yeast activity, sugar, dough formulation, and waiting time can all change proofing speed.

Separate over-proofing from lid transition

If both are plausible, test one variable at a time. For example, keep the dough formula and proof target the same while controlling the post-steam covered rest.

Check for condensation clues

Wet, pitted, or blotchy surfaces point toward direct moisture as an additional branch, not necessarily the only cause.

Record what happened before changing the recipe

Write down room temperature, proof state, steamer type, steam intensity, and post-steam handling. That makes the next batch an experiment instead of another guess.

Do not use a magic 5-minute rule. A short covered rest is a reasonable variable to control, but proofing state remains a major independent cause family. Good troubleshooting keeps both branches separate.
