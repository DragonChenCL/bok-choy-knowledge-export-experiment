Why did my bao collapse after steaming?

A collapsed bun is a symptom, not a diagnosis. Before adding proof time or changing the steamer, check what happened before steaming, when the volume loss appeared, and whether moisture was visible.

How to narrow it

Collapsed bao has more than one live branch.

1. Strong over-proof cues before steaming?

If the shaped buns were very puffy, fragile, spreading, or a finger indent failed to recover, over-proofing becomes the stronger branch. The next useful test is an earlier proof endpoint while keeping formula and steaming unchanged.

2. Wet spots, pits, or direct lid drips?

Visible moisture moves condensation higher. King Arthur specifically recommends controlling lid condensation because dripping water can make steamed buns wet; this is a different clue from collapse alone.

3. Neither clue is strong?

Do not pretend the cause is settled. Post-steam transition and hidden proof/structure weakness can both remain plausible.

CONFLICTED EVIDENCE

Some reputable guidance recommends a covered rest before opening the steamer; other experienced bao sources argue correct proofing matters more and immediate opening is fine. Treat lid-rest as an A/B variable after proofing is controlled, not as a universal explanation.

Evidence: King Arthur Baking — steamed bun setup and post-steam rest; Red House Spice — over-proof collapse and conflicting lid-rest view.

FAQ

Quick answers

Is collapsed bao always over-proofed?

No. Strong over-proof cues make it more likely, but visible condensation or a post-steam transition can point to different branches.

Should I always wait before opening the steamer lid?

Evidence and expert practice conflict. Control proofing first, then test lid-rest as one variable instead of assuming it is always required.

What should I change next batch?

Only the strongest live variable: for example an earlier final-proof endpoint or an anti-drip setup, while keeping the rest unchanged.
