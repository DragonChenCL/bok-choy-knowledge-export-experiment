Steam buns without a steamer

How to steam buns without a bamboo steamer

You do not need a bamboo basket to make steamed buns. You need a covered pot, a way to hold the buns above the water, and enough space for steam to circulate.

Quick answer

The easiest substitute is a pot + rack + heat-safe plate.

Put a small rack or trivet inside a lidded pot, add water below the rack, place a heat-safe plate on top, then arrange the buns on parchment squares. Keep the plate above the water and leave room around the edges for steam.

Evidence for this answer: The cooking principle is the same as standard steaming: keep the buns above the water, allow steam to circulate and prevent direct water contact. For dough and proofing behavior, see the steamed-bun methods from King Arthur Baking and Red House Spice.

Method 1

Pot + rack + plate

  1. Choose a pot with a lid and enough height for the buns to expand.
  2. Add a metal trivet, canning rack or other stable heat-safe support.
  3. Add water below the top of the rack.
  4. Set a heat-safe plate on the support and place parchment-lined buns on the plate.
  5. Cover and steam according to bun size and recipe.
Leave some open space around the plate so steam can rise freely instead of being blocked.
Method 2

Wok + trivet

A wok works well because the sloped sides give room for a trivet and plate above the water. Make sure the plate is stable and the lid closes without touching the buns.

Method 3

Heat-safe colander or insert

A metal colander or steaming insert can work if it fits securely inside the pot, stays above the water and leaves enough clearance for the lid. Avoid plastic unless it is explicitly rated for this heat and use.

Avoid these mistakes

Do not turn a steaming setup into a boiling setup.

  • Do not let rising water touch the buns.
  • Do not use an unstable bowl or rack that can tip as the water boils.
  • Do not block every steam path with an oversized plate.
  • Do not let a cloth used for condensation hang near a flame.
  • Do not use the microwave as a direct substitute for steaming raw yeast dough; it is better suited to reheating already-cooked buns.
When this advice may not apply

Know the boundary before you change the recipe.

Use only cookware that is heat-safe, stable and designed to tolerate steam. Do not improvise with plastic, glass that is not heat-safe, unstable bowls, or cloth near an exposed flame. If your pot is very shallow or the lid touches the buns, choose another setup rather than forcing the method.

FAQ

Common questions

Can I steam buns in a regular pot?

Yes. A covered pot can work if you create a stable platform that keeps the buns above the water and allows steam to circulate.

Can I use a plate to steam bao?

Yes, if the plate is heat-safe and sits securely above the water on a rack or trivet. Leave space for steam to move around it.

Can I microwave raw bao instead of steaming it?

A microwave is not a direct replacement for steaming raw yeast-leavened bao. It is much more useful for reheating buns that are already fully cooked.
