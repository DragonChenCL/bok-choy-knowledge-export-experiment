Bao troubleshooting

Why do my bao buns stick to parchment paper?

If the bottom of the bun tears away with the paper, do not immediately change the dough formula. First separate a liner problem from excess bottom moisture or a bun that was handled while very wet and delicate.

How to narrow it

Use the strongest clue first.

1. Check the contact surface

Steamed-bun guides commonly use parchment or another nonstick barrier, and some also recommend lightly oiling the steaming surface. Make sure the liner is actually suitable for steaming and extends under the bun.

2. Look for a moisture problem

If the bottom is soggy as well as stuck, investigate water level, condensation, and whether liquid is reaching the bun. Sticking alone and a waterlogged bottom are not the same failure.

3. Change one barrier variable

Do not simultaneously change dough hydration, steamer heat, and liner type. Test a different liner or a lightly oiled surface while keeping the bun batch the same.

Next-batch test

Steam matched buns from the same dough on two surfaces: your current parchment and one alternative nonstick liner or lightly oiled surface. Compare bottom moisture and release after steaming.

Evidence: Red House Spice — line the basket or brush oil; The Woks of Life — parchment and nonstick steaming surfaces.

FAQ

Quick answers

Should bao be steamed on parchment?

Yes, parchment squares or steamer liners are commonly used to prevent sticking, but the liner still needs to work well in a high-moisture steamer environment.

Does sticking mean my dough is too wet?

Not necessarily. A liner or condensation problem can make an otherwise good bun stick, so check the bottom texture before changing hydration.

Can I oil the steamer instead?

A thin layer of oil is another common anti-stick method, especially on metal surfaces.
