Dense Bao troubleshooting

Why are my bao buns not fluffy?

Start by separating a gas-production problem from a dough-structure problem. “Dense Bao” does not automatically mean dead yeast.

Step 1: did the dough rise before steaming?

Barely rose

Check yeast method and freshness, kitchen temperature, chilled ingredients, and major substitutions. Active dry and instant yeast are not always handled the same way.

Rose normally, still dense

Move beyond yeast. Final proof, dough development, flour / protein differences, hydration, and post-expansion collapse can all affect the finished texture.

Step 2: what kind of “not fluffy” is it?

Do not translate flour names too literally

A common troubleshooting mistake is assuming US all-purpose flour, UK plain flour, and Chinese Bao flour are exact equivalents. They are not. Protein level, gluten strength, starch properties, brand, and hydration response can differ.

Record measurable things: brand, flour category, protein % if printed, flour weight, liquid weight, and whether the raw dough felt dry, supple, tacky, or sticky.

Use one controlled change

Verify whether yeast / proofing is actually the problem

If the dough barely rose, solve that branch before changing flour.

If it rose, inspect structure

Compare flour, hydration, kneading, and final proof against the original recipe rather than replacing everything.

Change one variable

For example, keep flour and hydration the same while changing only final proof. Or keep proofing the same while returning to the recipe's original flour.

Important: there is no single universal “perfect Bao flour” across every regional style. The goal is to diagnose your formula relative to the texture it is designed to produce.
