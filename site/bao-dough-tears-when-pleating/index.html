Bao troubleshooting

Why does my bao dough tear when pleating?

Tearing while pleating is a dough-handling clue. Before adding more water or abandoning the batch, look at whether the dough is rough, dry at the edges, snapping back, or simply rolled too thin.

How to narrow it

Use the strongest clue first.

1. Check kneading and smoothness

If the dough still looks lumpy or tears easily, it may need more development before shaping. A smooth dough is a better baseline for judging hydration.

2. Prevent exposed pieces from drying

Unused dough and wrappers should stay covered. Dry edges can crack during pleating even when the center of the dough is correctly hydrated.

3. Check thickness before changing the recipe

If tearing happens only where the wrapper was rolled extremely thin, make the center slightly thicker and compare. Do not add water to the whole batch to fix one thin spot.

Next-batch test

Keep one dough piece covered for the entire shaping period and leave another exposed for your normal working time. Roll both to the same thickness. If only the exposed wrapper cracks, fix handling before hydration.

Evidence: The Woks of Life — dough smoothness, tearing and pleating; Red House Spice — keeping wrappers covered and preventing drying.

FAQ

Quick answers

Why does bao dough rip when I stretch it?

Rough under-developed dough, drying, insufficient rest, or an overly thin wrapper can all make pleating tear.

Should I add water to torn bao dough?

Only after you rule out drying and dough development. Adding water to an already balanced dough can create a new sticky-dough problem.

Why does my wrapper keep shrinking back?

Strong elastic resistance can mean the dough needs more relaxation before rolling and pleating.
