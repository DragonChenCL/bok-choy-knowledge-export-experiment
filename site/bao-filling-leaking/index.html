Bao troubleshooting

Why is my bao filling leaking out?

A leaking bao is not one problem. Filling can escape before steaming because the wrapper will not seal, or during steaming because the seal, wrapper thickness, or filling moisture cannot hold.

How to narrow it

Use the strongest clue first.

1. Check visible liquid first

Wet filling can soften the wrapper and make a clean seal much harder. If the filling is loose or visibly liquid, treat that as a separate branch before blaming the dough.

2. Reduce the closing load

Overfilling makes pleating and sealing harder. A bun that closes easily with less filling but leaks when packed full gives you a useful clue without changing the dough.

3. Inspect wrapper thickness and seal

Very thin areas can rupture even when the top is sealed. If the leak starts at one stretched point rather than the seam, wrapper distribution deserves the next test.

Next-batch test

Use the same dough and filling to make two matched buns: one with your usual fill weight and one with 20–25% less. If only the fuller bun leaks, fix load and sealing before changing the formula.

FAQ

Quick answers

Why does my bao open while steaming?

A weak seal, wet filling, overfilling, or a stretched thin spot can all make the bun open. Where the leak begins helps separate them.

Should bao filling be dry?

For easy sealing, the filling should be cohesive and not have obvious free liquid soaking the wrapper.

Does chilling filling help?

For fillings that become loose or difficult to handle, chilling can make assembly easier without changing the dough.
